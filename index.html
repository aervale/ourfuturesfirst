              About

              What is this?

              This is a student-run initiative to have the opinions of those at U.S. universities represented in regulation of the development of AI through collective design and signing of an open letter. We want as many opinions as possible, to give students, professors and anyone at academic institutions a larger say in the current climate of AI development.

              Why isn't the letter out now?

              We want to move together as one and sign at the same time, to reach as many people as possible on the day it comes out, and to gather feedback beforehand. We will change the contents of the letter until launch day, and we publish the draft so you can see it change. The current wording is here.

              Please pass this along if you believe in our mission and want more representation in our future.

              What happens on launch day?

              Everyone on the list gets contacted at the same time to spread and sign the letter. The letter and its signatures are then published.

              Who can take part?

              Anyone with a U.S. university email: undergraduates, graduate students, postdocs, faculty and staff.

              Who are we?

              This was started by a group of students at MIT and continues to be entirely student-run. We have been concerned about the development of superintelligence and feel it is time for us to speak out.

              What is a campus organizer?

              Someone who has offered to help with the letter at their university. A university can have several.

              If you volunteer, we connect you with other groups of campus organizers at your university, which means sharing your name with them, and contact you by phone or text about next steps. A phone number is required. Organizers are listed by name on the public list and marked as organizers.

              What happens to my information?

              We use your email just to confirm your affiliation with your university and to tell you when the letter is ready. Your first name and last initial are on the public list only if you choose to show them. Poll answers are shown as totals. Your answer to “What concerns you most about AI?” isn't displayed publicly. The privacy page has the details.

                Privacy

                Last updated November 24, 2026.

                Who we are

                Our Futures First is a project run by students at U.S. universities. It is collecting interest in an open letter on AI. You can reach us at contact@ourfuturesfirst.org.

                What we collect

                What you enter in the form: your name, your university email, your role and year or title, your major or field, a phone number if you volunteer as a campus organizer, your answers to the poll questions, your written answer, and whether you offered to help.

                We also record whether and when you confirmed your email, and, if you arrived through someone's share link, that their link brought you. The services that host this website and its database receive your IP address and basic browser details when your browser loads the page. A code saved in your browser lets you reopen your own entry from the same device. We do not use advertising trackers, and the only counting we do is described under Analytics below.

                What is public

                If you choose to show your name, your first name and last initial appear on the public list with your university. If you also choose to show them, so do your year or title and your major. If you volunteered as a campus organizer, the list says so. Answers to the poll questions, roles, years and majors are shown only as totals, and each university is shown with its number of sign-ups.

                Your email, phone number and written answer are never shown. Anything that has been public may have been copied by others, and we can't delete their copies.

                How we use it

                To confirm that you belong to a U.S. university, to contact you about the letter, to count sign-ups and show totals, and to decide what goes in the letter. If you volunteer as a campus organizer, your name is shared with the other campus organizers at your university.

                Phone numbers

                We ask for a phone number only if you volunteer as a campus organizer. Organizers are contacted by phone or text, by the students running this project, about the letter and organizing on their campus. We do not sell phone numbers or share them with anyone for marketing.

                Inviting friends

                If you ask us to email friends, we keep the addresses you type, send each one a single invitation with your link, and use them for nothing else. You can invite up to 20 people.

                Who we share it with

                Only the services that run the site for us: the company that hosts this website, Supabase for the database and sign-in emails, an email delivery service, and Cloudflare, which checks that a sign-up isn't automated. Google Fonts and jsDelivr deliver fonts and code to your browser and so see your IP address. We do not sell personal information. We may disclose information if the law requires it.

                How long we keep it

                Until the letter project ends, or until you remove yourself or ask us to delete it. An entry whose email is never confirmed is not counted or shown, and is taken off the main list after 7 days.

                Your choices

                You can change your entry or remove yourself with “Edit my answers” and “Remove me” on the form, from the browser you signed up in. Removing yourself deletes your sign-up. To see, correct or delete what we hold about you from anywhere else, email contact@ourfuturesfirst.org.

                Analytics and advertising cookies

                We use no cookies for analytics or advertising, and no pixels that follow you to other websites. We do not build profiles of visitors, and we do not sell or share information for advertising.

                We do count two things about sharing, so we can see how the letter spreads. When someone opens a personal share link, we record that the link was opened and, if the link says so, whether it was sent by text, WhatsApp, Threads or email. When you press Copy or one of the share buttons on your own confirmation page, we record which button. Each record is tied to the share link, not to the person who opened it: we keep no cookie, no IP address and no device details with it.

                What your browser does keep is only what the site needs to work: a code that lets you reopen your own entry, the code of the share link you arrived through, and, after you open the link in our email, a sign-in for that confirmation. The spam check from Cloudflare may set a cookie of its own while it confirms you are not a bot. Because nothing here is optional, there is no cookie banner to accept.

                Do Not Track

                We do not track you across other websites, so this site behaves the same way whatever your browser's Do Not Track setting is.

                Children

                This site is for people at universities. It is not directed to children under 13.

                Changes

                If this page changes, we will update the date at the top.

                Help us edit our message

                This is what we have written for the letter so far. We will update it as your answers come in. On launch day, you'll see the final text and then decide whether to sign.

                At academic institutions, we have long been taught to prize the enterprise of thought. But today, that enterprise is being taken over, automated at ever-increasing rates by systems no one can yet reliably control. We believe superintelligence must be independently tested and shown to be safe before AI companies race further, or the human future itself is at risk. We ask the US government and AI companies to put our futures first: to lead negotiations on international rules that pace the development of superintelligence.
